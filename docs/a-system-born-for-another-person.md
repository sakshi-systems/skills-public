# A system born for another person

The family grows working systems for people outside it — a system administrator, a photographer — and hands them over. Each is born as a seed: one repository, in an organization of its own, holding a single instruction file an agent opens and works from. The person and the agent grow the rest together. The family sees the system until the organization is handed over, and after that sees nothing of it.

## The seed

A seed is a `CLAUDE.md`, an `AGENTS.md` that links to it so any agent reads the same, and a short `README`. It carries nothing of the family's machinery: no skills, no heads, no buffers, no names or paths of ours. What it carries is the discipline the family works by, translated into the person's own work and words:

- **The first conversation comes before any structure.** While the repository holds only the seed, the agent changes nothing and advises nothing. It asks the person, one short question at a time, how they see their own work, what they already have, what costs them most, what the agent may do alone and what never — and only then proposes the sections they need, and creates them on their yes. A ready scheme laid over someone's work is the agent's view, not theirs.
- **The repository first, the world after.** A change to the infrastructure, a post, a page of the site is written and agreed in the repository before it goes out.
- **What is lives over what is written.** The agent checks the record against the live state before acting, and a mismatch is a finding to name, not to correct silently.
- **One home for a fact; secrets never in it.**
- **The person's word decides.** Nothing irreversible, nothing published, nothing sent without it.
- **A correction is a rule.** What the person corrects is written down at once, in their words, as the rule behind it — for a photographer, the voice she writes in.
- **Agents talk through the repository**, by letters as files, and never speak for one another.

The seed is written for the person it is born for, in their language, at the height of their work: a photographer is not asked about commits.

## Before and after the handover

Until the organization is handed over, the family can read what grows. After it, the content is the person's, and the family keeps only the seed's birth: the files as they were handed over, with the date, the commit and who they were for, in its own repository. What the person and the agent wrote afterwards is never there, and never copied there.

## What comes back

The only way back is the person's own musafir, and only if the person allows it: what the seed lacked, where the first conversation asked the wrong thing, which rule did not hold. That experience, never the person's content, is what makes the next seed better. A birth is the start of a cycle, not a gift that ends at the handover.

## Not the role seeds

The family's role seeds — the razor, the navigator, the builder, the architect, the musafir — are a different thing: they are the family's own, raised by Sakshi on a project's pull request, and they stay with the family. A system born for another person is that person's from its first answer.
